
# ADJECTIVE

## 1. Definition of Adjective

An adjective is a word used to describe or qualify a **noun or pronoun**.

Examples:
- He is a **good** boy.
- She is **intelligent**.
- The **beautiful** flower looks attractive.

---

## 2. Position of Adjectives

An adjective can be used in two positions:

### A. Attributive Use

When an adjective is placed **before a noun**, it is called attributive use.

Example:
- He is a **good** boy.
- She is an **intelligent** girl.

### B. Predicative Use

When an adjective is placed **after a linking verb**, it is called predicative use.

Example:
- The boy is **good**.
- She looks **beautiful**.
- He seems **intelligent**.

**Important:** Predicative adjectives generally follow linking verbs such as *is, am, are, was, were, look, seem, become, feel* etc.

### Adjectives Generally Used Only in Predicative Position

Certain adjectives are generally used after linking verbs and not directly before nouns.

| Adjective | Example |
|---|---|
| Afraid | He is afraid. |
| Alone | He is alone. |
| Asleep | The baby is asleep. |
| Awake | He is awake. |
| Alike | Both sisters look alike. |
| Alive | The fish is alive. |
| Afloat | The boat is afloat. |
| Alert | The guard is alert. |
| Aware | He is aware of the situation. |
| Ashamed | He is ashamed of his behaviour. |
| Fond | She is fond of music. |
| Eager | He is eager to learn. |

**Examples:**

1. Incorrect: I saw three **alive men** lying on the road.  
   Correct: I saw three **men alive** lying on the road.

2. Correct: He is afraid.  
   Incorrect: He is an afraid man.

3. Correct: He is a frightened man.  
   *Frightened* can be used attributively as well as predicatively.

4. Correct: He is alone.  
   Incorrect: He is an alone person.

5. Correct: He is a lonely person.  
   *Lonely* can be used attributively.

6. Correct: Both the sisters look alike.

7. Correct: The baby fell asleep.

> **Important distinction:**
> - Afraid = feeling fear.
> - Frightened = feeling fear.
> - Alone = without anyone else.
> - Lonely = feeling sad because of being alone.

**Exam Tip:** Remember the common predicative-only adjectives using this list:
Afraid, Asleep, Alive, Alone, Awake, Alike, Afloat, Aware.

## 3. Degrees of Adjectives

Adjectives have three degrees of comparison:

1. **Positive Degree:** Expresses a quality without comparison.
2. **Comparative Degree:** Compares two persons or things.
3. **Superlative Degree:** Expresses the highest or lowest degree among three or more.

Example:

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| Tall | Taller | Tallest |
| Good | Better | Best |
| Bad | Worse | Worst |
| Much | More | Most |
| Many | More | Most |
| Little | Less | Least |
| Few | Fewer | Fewest |

### Much, Many, Little and Few

| Word | Used with | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Much | Uncountable nouns | More | Most |
| Many | Countable nouns | More | Most |
| Little | Uncountable nouns | Less | Least |
| Few | Countable nouns | Fewer | Fewest |

**Important: Less vs Lesser**

- Less is the comparative form of little.
- Lesser is also a comparative adjective, generally meaning *smaller in importance, size or degree*.
- Both *less* and *lesser* are comparative forms; **lesser is not the positive degree**.

### Use of Less and Lesser

**Less** is commonly used with *than* in comparisons.

Example:
- This evil is less harmful than the other one.

**Lesser** is commonly used before a noun and in the expression *the lesser of the two*.

Example:
- This is the lesser of the two evils.

**Incorrect:** This is less/lesser than the two evils.  
**Correct:** This is the lesser of the two evils.

> **Remember:** The natural expression is *the lesser of the two evils*. Do not treat the choice between less and lesser as a strict rule based only on the presence of *of* or *than*.

## 4. Confusing Uses of Much, Many, Little and Few

### A. Much vs Many

- Much → Uncountable nouns.
- Many → Countable nouns.

Example:

He has as much / many as fifty cars.  
**Correct:** Many — *cars* is countable.

### B. Less vs Fewer

- Less → Uncountable nouns.
- Fewer → Countable nouns.

Example:

No less / fewer than fifty students were present.  
**Correct:** Fewer — *students* is countable.

### C. Many vs Much

Of the millions of stars in the sky, how many / much are there?  
**Correct:** Many — *stars* is countable.

### D. Few vs Little

Little / Few students attended the seminar.  
**Correct:** Few — *students* is countable.

> **Quick Revision Trick:**
> - Much / Less / Little → Uncountable
> - Many / Fewer / Few → Countable
> - More / Most → Both countable and uncountable
