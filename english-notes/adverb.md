# ADVERB

## 1. Definition of Adverb

An **Adverb** is a word that modifies or describes a **Verb, Adjective, or another Adverb**.

> **Examples:**
>
> - He runs **quickly**. → Modifies a Verb.
> - She is **very** beautiful. → Modifies an Adjective.
> - He runs **extremely** fast. → Modifies another Adverb.

---

## 2. Types of Adverbs

| Type | Meaning | Examples | Example Sentence |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Adverb of Time** | When? | Today, Yesterday, Now, Soon, Already | I will come **tomorrow**. |
| **Adverb of Place** | Where? | Here, There, Everywhere, Outside, Upstairs | He is standing **outside**. |
| **Adverb of Frequency** | How often? | Always, Usually, Often, Sometimes, Never | She **always** speaks politely. |
| **Adverb of Manner** | How? | Slowly, Quickly, Carefully, Easily, Beautifully | He drives **carefully**. |

### Memory Trick

> - **Time** → When?
> - **Place** → Where?
> - **Frequency** → How often?
> - **Manner** → How?

---

## 3. Rule: Position of Adverb of Frequency

> **Rule 1:** Adverbs of Frequency generally come **BEFORE the Main Verb**.

**Examples:**

- He **always** works hard.
- She **often** visits the temple.
- I **never** tell lies.

**Exam Tip:**

Remember the basic pattern:

- Main Verb → Adverb + Main Verb
- Helping Verb → Helping Verb + Adverb + Main Verb

---

## 4. MPT Rule (Adverb Order)

When multiple adverbs of **Manner, Place and Time** are used together in a sentence, the general order is:

> **MPT = Manner → Place → Time** (Remember as eMPTy)

| Order | Type | Question |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| 1 | **Manner (M)** | How? |
| 2 | **Place (P)** | Where? |
| 3 | **Time (T)** | When? |

### Examples

1. He worked **hard (M)** in the office **(P)** yesterday **(T)**.
2. She sang **beautifully (M)** on the stage **(P)** last night **(T)**.
3. They played **happily (M)** in the park **(P)** yesterday **(T)**.

**Incorrect:** He worked yesterday in the office hard.
**Correct:** He worked hard in the office yesterday.

## 5. Other Kinds of Adverbs

### A. Adverbs of Degree or Quantity

These adverbs indicate **how much or to what degree/extent** an action or quality exists.

| Adverb | Example |
|:---|:---|
| Fully | I fully agree with you. |
| Too | The tea is too hot. |
| Quite | She is quite intelligent. |
| Very | He is very honest. |
| Almost | I have almost finished my work. |

> **Question to remember:** How much? / To what extent?

### B. Adverbs of Affirmation and Negation

These adverbs express **confirmation, agreement, certainty or denial**.

| Type | Examples | Example Sentence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Affirmation | Yes, Certainly, Surely, Definitely | She will certainly come. |
| Negation | No, Not, Never | He will never lie. |

> **Remember:**
> - Affirmation → Positive statement.
> - Negation → Negative statement.

### C. Adverbs of Reason

These adverbs indicate **the reason, result or consequence** of an action or situation.

| Adverb | Example |
|:---|:---|
| Therefore | He was ill; therefore, he stayed home. |
| Hence | He worked hard; hence, he succeeded. |
| Consequently | It rained heavily; consequently, the match was cancelled. |

> **Key Concept:** Therefore, Hence and Consequently generally express a result or consequence.

## 6. AAA Concept (Adjective – Adverb – Abstract Noun)

Many words can be converted into three forms:

- **Adjective:** Describes a noun.
- **Adverb:** Modifies a verb, adjective or another adverb.
- **Abstract Noun:** Represents a quality, state or feeling.

| Adjective | Adverb | Abstract Noun |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Kind | Kindly | Kindness |
| Honest | Honestly | Honesty |
| Careful | Carefully | Carefulness |
| Careless | Carelessly | Carelessness |
| Faithful | Faithfully | Faithfulness |
| Angry | Angrily | Anger |
| True | Truly | Truth |
| Truthful | Truthfully | Truthfulness |

### Examples

1. **Adjective:** He is an honest man.
2. **Adverb:** He behaves honestly.
3. **Abstract Noun:** Honesty is the best policy.

## 7. Confusing Adverbs and Their Usage

### A. Hard vs Hardly

| Word | Meaning | Usage |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Hard** | With great effort | Adverb of Manner |
| **Hardly** | Almost not / scarcely | Adverb of Degree |

**Examples:**

- He works **hard**. (He puts in great effort.)
- He **hardly** works. (He almost does not work.)

> **Important Rule:** Adverbs of Frequency generally come before the main verb.

**Negative Usage:**

- Incorrect: He hardly has **no** work.
- Correct: He hardly has **any** work.

> **Remember:** Hardly is already a negative word. Avoid using another negative word (No/Not) with it.

---

### B. Fast vs Fastly

> **Remember:** There is no standard English adverb *Fastly*. The word **Fast** can function as both an Adjective and an Adverb.

| Usage | Example |
|:---|:---|
| Adjective | He drives a **fast** car. |
| Adverb | He drives **fast**. |
| Adverb | He ran **fast**. |

---

### C. Concept: Stative Verbs vs Action Verbs

- **Stative Verbs:** Express a state, condition, feeling or sense. An adjective is generally used after linking/stative verbs to describe the subject.
- **Action Verbs:** Express an action. An adverb is generally used to describe how the action is performed.

> **Important:** The choice depends on the meaning and function of the verb, not merely whether it is called a stative or action verb.

---

### D. Bitter vs Bitterly

| Word | Meaning / Usage |
|:---|:---|
| **Bitter** | Having a bitter taste |
| **Bitterly** | In a very bad, intense or painful manner |

**Examples:**

1. The food tasted **bitter**. (Taste = sensory linking verb)
2. He was crying **bitterly**. (Crying = Action Verb)
3. He was **bitterly** disappointed. (Bitterly modifies the adjective disappointed.)

> **Rule:** Use *Bitter* for bitter taste and *Bitterly* to express intensity or suffering.

---

### E. Sweet vs Sweetly

| Word | Meaning / Usage |
|:---|:---|
| **Sweet** | Having a sweet taste |
| **Sweetly** | In a pleasant, melodious or gentle manner |

**Examples:**

1. Custard tastes **sweet**. (Taste = sensory linking verb)
2. She sang **sweetly**. (Sang = Action Verb)

> **Rule:** Use *Sweet* for taste and *Sweetly* for a pleasant or melodious manner.

---

### F. Late vs Lately

| Word | Meaning | Usage |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Late** | Not on time | Adjective / Adverb |
| **Lately** | Recently / Of late | Adverb of Time |

**Examples:**

1. He is **late**. (Adjective – describes the subject)
2. He came **late**. (Adverb – describes the verb)
3. I haven't talked to him **lately**. (Recently)
4. **Lately**, I haven't been to the movies. (Recently)

> **Remember:**
> - Late = Not on time.
> - Lately = Recently = Of late.

---

### G. Respectfully vs Respectively

| Word | Meaning | Usage |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Respectfully** | With respect and dignity | Shows the manner of an action |
| **Respectively** | In the same order or sequence | Shows corresponding order |

**Examples:**

1. When the guest arrived, the audience stood up **respectfully**. (With respect)
2. Ram and Mohan, aged 4 and 5 **respectively**, are good at playing. (In the same order)

> **Remember:**
> - Respectfully = With respect.
> - Respectively = In the given order.

---

### H. Good vs Well

| Word | Part of Speech | Usage |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Good** | Adjective | Describes a noun or subject |
| **Well** | Adverb | Describes how an action is performed |
| **Well** | Adjective | Generally describes health or physical condition |

**Examples:**

1. He is **good**. (Adjective)
2. He is **well**. (Adjective – health condition)
3. He behaved **well**. (Adverb – describes the verb behaved)

> **Important Rule:**
> - Good is an adjective, not an adverb.
> - Well is generally used as an adverb but can also function as an adjective when referring to health.

---

### I. Pretty vs Prettily

**Pretty** has two important meanings:
1. Beautiful or attractive.
2. To a considerable degree / Fairly / Quite.

**Prettily** means in a beautiful, attractive or pleasing manner.

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Pretty** | Beautiful | She is a pretty girl. |
| **Pretty** | To a considerable degree | The question is pretty difficult. |
| **Prettily** | In a beautiful manner | She decorated the room prettily. |

> **Remember:**
> - Pretty = Beautiful / Fairly / Quite.
> - Prettily = In a beautiful manner.

## 8. Adverb and Inversion

**Inversion** means changing the normal sentence order by placing the auxiliary/helping verb before the subject.

> **Normal Structure:** Subject + Auxiliary Verb + Main Verb  
> **Inversion:** Adverb/Adverbial Phrase + Auxiliary Verb + Subject + Main Verb

### Important Rule

Certain negative or restrictive adverbs cause inversion **when they are placed at the beginning of a sentence**.

| Adverb / Phrase | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| Not | Negative |
| Never | At no time |
| Hardly | Almost not |
| Scarcely | Almost not |
| Seldom | Rarely |
| Little | Not much / Not at all |
| So | To such an extent |
| Too | To an excessive degree |
| Under no circumstances | In no situation |

### A. Never

- He **never helps** me. (No inversion)
- **Never does he help** me. (Inversion)
- **Never have I met** a person like him. (Inversion)

### B. Hardly

- He **hardly attended** the classes. (No inversion)
- **Hardly did he attend** the classes. (Inversion)

### C. So

- His voice was **so hoarse** that he couldn't speak a single word. (No inversion)
- **So hoarse was his voice** that he couldn't speak a single word. (Inversion)

### D. Not

- He could **not speak** a single word. (No inversion)
- **Not a single word could he speak.** (Inversion)

### E. Too

- He was **too weak** to do the work. (No inversion)
- **Too weak was he** to do the work. (Inversion)

### F. Little

> Little causes inversion when it is used as an adverb at the beginning of a sentence.

- He **knew little** about the incident. (No inversion)
- **Little did he know** about the incident. (Inversion)

### G. Under No Circumstances

- **Under no circumstances will I help him.** (Inversion)

> **Important:** When these negative or restrictive expressions are placed at the beginning of a sentence, use inversion. If the sentence starts with a normal subject, inversion is generally not required.

## 9. Position of Adverbs – Only, Even and Enough

### A. Use of ONLY

The position of **Only** is very important because changing its position can change the meaning of the sentence.

> **Rule:** Place ONLY immediately before the word or phrase that you want to restrict or emphasise.

| Incorrect Sentence | Correct Sentence | Explanation |
|:---|:---|:---|
| He only spoke and everyone was silent. | **Only he** spoke and everyone was silent. | Only refers to He. |
| She only answered two questions. | She answered **only two questions**. | Only refers to two questions. |
| The only death of her son was shocking. | The death of her **only son** was shocking. | Only refers to son. |
| She only eats strawberries for dinner. | She eats **only strawberries** for dinner. | Only refers to strawberries. |
| They only work when they need money. | They work **only when they need money**. | Only refers to the condition. |

> **Remember:** Only should be placed immediately before the word or phrase it modifies.

### B. Use of EVEN

**Rule:** Even is generally placed immediately before the word or phrase that it emphasises.

**Example:**

- Incorrect: He can even do this work.
- Correct: He can do **even this work**.

> Even this work = Emphasises the difficulty or unexpected nature of the work.

### C. Use of ENOUGH

**Enough** can function as both an Adjective/Determiner and an Adverb.

| Usage | Rule | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Adjective / Determiner | Enough comes before a noun. | We haven't got **enough money** to complete the project. |
| Adverb | Enough comes after an adjective or adverb. | She is **smart enough** to handle the situation. |

**Important Rules:**

1. When Enough modifies an adjective, it is placed **after the adjective**.
2. Enough is followed by the **positive degree** of an adjective, not the comparative or superlative degree.

**Examples:**

- She is smart **enough** to handle the situation.
- He is tall **enough** to be selected for the basketball team.
- Incorrect: He is wiser enough to do the work on time.
- Correct: He is **wise enough** to do the work on time.

> **Quick Revision:**
> - Enough + Noun → Enough money.
> - Adjective + Enough → Smart enough.
> - Positive Degree → Wise enough (Not wiser enough).
